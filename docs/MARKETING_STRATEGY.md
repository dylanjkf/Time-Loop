# TIME LOOP — Go-to-Market & Marketing Strategy

This document assumes the product described in `docs/GAME_DESIGN_DOCUMENT.md`, `docs/ROADMAP.md`, `docs/PUZZLE_GENERATION.md`, and `docs/TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE.md`: a built, submission-ready Unity/C# premium puzzle game for iOS and Android, ~23 hand-verified levels across 4 worlds proving out a 150+ level campaign format, a Wordle-style free-forever Daily Time Loop Challenge with deterministic worldwide seeding and an arrow-emoji share card, a solver-verified Infinite Mode, and an ethical one-time-purchase model ($4.99–$7.99) with zero pay-to-win and zero mid-puzzle ads. Every recommendation below is written against those specific, already-implemented systems — not generic mobile-marketing advice retrofitted onto a puzzle game.

## 1. Positioning Statement

Time Loop is for the adult puzzle-game buyer who already owns Monument Valley, The Room, Baba Is You, or Braid on their phone — someone who reliably pays $5–$10 upfront for a finite, beautifully made puzzle experience and resents energy timers, ad interruptions, and gacha monetization enough to actively seek out games that don't have them — and who also, separately, has a Wordle-shaped hole in their daily routine: one quick, shareable puzzle ritual before or after they check the news. Time Loop sits on two shelves at once — the premium "designer puzzle box" shelf next to The Room and Monument Valley, and the free daily-habit shelf next to Wordle and the NYT Games app — and is one of the only titles that credibly occupies both, because the campaign and the Daily Challenge are the same engine wearing two hats. The one-sentence promise: **the only other player you'll ever need is your own past self, and there's a fresh proof of that, free, every single day.**

## 2. Pre-Launch Strategy (T-8 Weeks to Launch)

The central pre-launch bet is that the core mechanic is unusually visual and explainable in under 10 seconds — the entire Level 1-01 teaching moment in the GDD (§4.1: reach the switch *or* the door, never both, until the ghost holds the switch for you) *is* a marketing asset before it is anything else. Pre-launch strategy is built around getting that specific "wait — I can use myself?" beat in front of the right eyes repeatedly, in escalating form, mirroring the way the campaign itself escalates World 1 → World 4.

### T-8: Foundations live
- **App Store "Coming Soon"** page live (Apple's pre-order mechanism: users tap Pre-Order once, the app auto-downloads on release day with zero further action — this is the direct mobile equivalent of a Steam wishlist and should be treated with the same seriousness; enable it the moment a build can pass review, even weeks before the real launch date).
- **Google Play pre-registration** live, with the pre-registration reward slot pointed at something real and cheap to grant (e.g., one free cosmetic Timeline theme unlock at launch, or a Daily Challenge streak-shield item) — pre-reg reward campaigns measurably raise sign-up volume on Play.
- Press kit v1 assembled: logo, 6-8 annotated screenshots (one per major world aesthetic per GDD §10 — warm white World 1 through near-monochrome World 4), a 15-second raw gameplay GIF of the Level 1-01 "wait" moment specifically, and one-paragraph pitch text drawn directly from the positioning statement above.
- Devlog channel stood up (a personal dev-focused blog/itch.io devlog *plus* a TikTok/YouTube Shorts/Instagram Reels account) — short-form video is the primary pre-launch channel because the mechanic is inherently a visual reveal, not a text pitch.

### T-7 to T-5: The "mechanic reveal ladder"
Post 2x/week short-form clips (15-30 seconds, no voiceover needed — the visual "wait" beat carries itself), each isolating exactly one cooperation pattern from GDD §4.3, released in the *same order the campaign teaches them*:
1. Hold-and-pass (World 1) — the simplest possible "oh" moment, used as the account's first-ever post so first-time viewers get the cleanest version of the hook.
2. Two-switch doors (World 1) — "one door, two switches, one me."
3. Relay carrying / timed windows (World 2) — introduces the idea that timing, not just position, matters.
4. Multi-ghost choreography (World 3) — the "wait, there are *three* of me now" escalation clip, which tends to be the most shareable because it's the most visually surprising.

This ladder does double duty: it is the pre-launch marketing calendar, and it is a low-cost way to demo-test which mechanic beat gets the best engagement before committing paid-promotion budget (there is none assumed here, but organic engagement data should still steer which clip gets reused in the launch trailer, see §3).

### T-6: Press and creator outreach
Target puzzle-specific press and creators over generalist gaming outlets, because this genre has an unusually dedicated specialist press: AppUnwrapper (the most puzzle/escape-room-literate mobile blog in the category), TouchArcade, Pocket Gamer, 148Apps, and indie-puzzle-focused YouTubers/streamers who've covered Baba Is You, The Room series, or A Monster's Expedition. Send a private TestFlight (iOS) / Play internal-testing (Android) build, the press kit, and a two-paragraph pitch that leads with the mechanic, not the studio — reviewers in this niche have explicitly said they're fatigued by generic "hidden object" and match-3 pitches, and respond to a genuinely original verb ("cooperate with your own recorded past self") far better than a features list.

### T-5 to T-3: Public beta / TestFlight feedback loop
Open a public TestFlight external testing group (and a Play open/closed testing track) with specific, narrow questions attached rather than "let us know what you think" — the GDD is explicit that Level 1-01 must teach the entire mechanic with zero text inside 60 seconds (§4.1's "60-second rule"), so beta feedback should be instrumented to answer:
- Did you understand what the ghost was without being told? (the single highest-risk unknown in the whole product)
- Where in World 1→2 did difficulty feel like it jumped rather than climbed?
- Was the Daily Challenge arrow-emoji share text something you'd actually paste into a chat, or did it need a screenshot instead? (this directly informs §4's share-card decisions)
- Any confusion around the free-tier wall (end of World 2's first 30 levels)?

Recruit beta testers partly from the devlog audience already built in T-7/T-6 — this seeds a group of people who will plausibly be your first authentic Daily Challenge posters at launch (see §3's day-one seeding).

### T-4 to T-2: Lock and submit
Finalize the launch trailer (§3) and distribute review codes/keys to press and creators under an embargo that lifts at or just before launch, so coverage lands in the first 72 hours rather than trickling in. Submit the build to App Store Review and Google Play Review with buffer for at least one rejection/resubmission cycle — since this is a from-scratch submission with no prior review history, do not submit for the first time inside a two-week launch window.

### T-1: Final countdown
Confirm both stores' approval, lock the release date, and push a final "48 hours" devlog/short-form post. If Apple's pre-order date has been running, this is also the point to line up any last co-ordinated day-one press embargo lifts.

## 3. Launch Week Strategy

### The App Store / Google Play editorial pitch
Time Loop is unusually well-shaped for editorial features, and the pitch should say so explicitly rather than hoping editors notice:

- **Apple's stated editorial taste has shifted toward "quality over F2P"** — a one-time $4.99–$7.99 purchase with no ads mid-play and no pay-to-win is exactly the shape Apple's "New Games We Love" and "Editors' Choice" placements have favored over energy-timer F2P in recent cycles. Say this plainly in the App Store Connect editorial-consideration notes field, and separately via any existing Apple developer relations contact: *original mechanic, premium one-time purchase, zero interstitials during play.*
- **The Daily Challenge is the evergreen-engagement hook editorial teams specifically look for.** Apple and Google both feature "daily habit" games (Wordle-likes, NYT Games, Two Dots' daily challenge) in dedicated slots because they justify a recurring feature placement, not a one-time push — pitch the Daily Challenge as a standalone hook in addition to the campaign, since it's genuinely free forever and costless for an editor to recommend without a "but you have to pay" caveat.
- **The five built VFX systems are a visual-hook asset for editorial video/banner placements** (`GhostTrailEffect`, `TimeRewindEffect`, `TimelineSplitEffect`, `LevelCompleteEffect`, `ClockDistortionEffect` per Technical Architecture §4.8/§10) — supply editors with clean, high-frame-rate capture of the `TimelineSplitEffect` moment specifically (the screen visibly forking as the ghost peels away), since that's the single most screenshot/video-bannerable beat in the game.
- **Accessibility as an editorial angle, not just a feature list bullet.** The colorblind-safe shape-coded interactables, on-screen D-pad alternative, and the "no countdown pressure" manual-split toggle (GDD §12) are exactly the kind of inclusive-design detail both storefronts' editorial teams now call out explicitly — mention it in the pitch, don't bury it in patch notes.

Submit the editorial pitch 2-4 weeks ahead of launch on both platforms (features are planned on a lead-time editorial calendar, not decided reactively on release day), and treat the T-6/T-4 press outreach in §2 as a second, parallel track feeding the same story to human press rather than an algorithmic feature slot.

### Launch trailer structure (30-45 seconds, beat by beat)
1. **0:00-0:04 — The impossible room.** A single player character standing between a switch and a locked door, clearly unable to reach both. No text. Let the geometry ask the question.
2. **0:04-0:10 — The "wait" moment.** Timer hits zero, `TimelineSplitEffect` plays, a translucent ghost appears standing on the switch. Beat of stillness before the player resets — this is the one moment in the whole trailer that must not be rushed.
3. **0:10-0:20 — The escalation montage.** Fast cuts, rising music: World 2 relay-carry, a timed-window dash, then a World 3 three-ghost room resolving together. Each cut is 1-2 seconds; the point is to communicate "and it keeps getting stranger" without explaining any of it.
4. **0:20-0:28 — The payoff.** A hard puzzle resolves, `LevelCompleteEffect` bloom plays, stars fill in (★★★). This is the emotional release beat after the tension of the montage.
5. **0:28-0:35 — Daily Challenge reveal.** Cut to a phone UI: the day's result card (`TIME LOOP #365 · Solved in 3 timelines · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐`) being shared into a messaging app. Overlay text: *"One free puzzle. Every day. Forever."*
6. **0:35-0:42 — The value proposition, stated plainly.** Title card + a single clean line: *"150+ levels. One-time purchase. No ads. No subscriptions."* — the pricing ethics are a selling point for this specific audience and should be said in the trailer, not hidden in the App Store description.
7. **0:42-0:45 — Logo lockup, platform badges, release date/price.**

### Day-one community seeding
Because `DailySeedGenerator` derives the same seed from the UTC calendar date for every player worldwide (Technical Architecture §4.7), the very first Daily Challenge is *identical* for every single player on Earth — this is a real, usable launch-day asset, not just a retention feature. Concretely:
- Recruit 5-10 authentic accounts from the beta-tester pool built in §2 (not bots, not paid amplification) to post their actual first-day result card the moment their timezone hits daily reset, using the in-game share button.
- Because timezones stagger, players in earlier timezones post results before players in later ones even wake up — this creates a real, organic "people are already posting about this, what is it" curiosity gap the moment US/EU audiences see it, exactly the mechanism that made Wordle's early grid-sharing spread before most people had played it.
- Prepare (but do not pre-fill with fake data) a simple pinned thread/post template on X/Threads/Reddit (r/iosgaming, r/AndroidGaming, r/puzzles) inviting people to reply with their own Day 1 card — the goal on day one is *visible momentum*, not volume.

## 4. The Daily Challenge as the Core Retention/Growth Engine

The Daily Challenge is the single highest-leverage growth lever in this product, for three reasons specific to how it's actually built:

1. **It is genuinely free forever with no ads inside it** (GDD §11, Technical Architecture §4.7) — it is the only feature in the game a press outlet, streamer, or curious friend can try with zero cost and zero commitment, which makes it the lowest-friction thing anyone will ever recommend Time Loop for. A reviewer who won't spend $5 on a mobile game will still open a free daily puzzle.
2. **Every player worldwide gets the identical puzzle**, because `DailyChallengeManager` always calls `ProceduralGenerator.Generate` with `DailySeedGenerator.SeedForToday()` at a fixed difficulty tier — this is what makes a shared result *mean* something. Comparability is the entire mechanism behind Wordle-style virality: a score is only interesting to share if the person reading it solved (or is about to attempt) the exact same puzzle. Time Loop already has this property engineered in; the marketing job is to make sure the sharing surface is as frictionless as possible.
3. **The share text is generated by the game itself, for free, at zero incremental cost per share** — `MoveNotation` encodes a solution as arrow-emoji, and `ShareCardGenerator` composes it into a line like `TIME LOOP #365 · Solved in 3 timelines · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐`. Every single voluntary share is a piece of self-explanatory marketing collateral posted by someone else, at no cost to the studio — this is structurally the same growth loop as Wordle's colored-square grid, adapted to this game's own notation (arrows + star rating + timeline count instead of green/yellow/gray squares).

### How to instrument and encourage sharing
- **Make the share button a native OS share sheet, not just copy-to-clipboard.** A one-tap copy is fine as a fallback, but routing `ShareCardGenerator`'s output through the platform share sheet (iMessage, WhatsApp, X, Reddit, Instagram Stories) removes a manual paste step and meaningfully increases completion-to-share conversion.
- **Ship an image-card variant alongside the plain-text one.** Instagram Stories and TikTok favor image shares over pasted text; a small designed card (dark background, the arrow-emoji sequence large and centered, star rating, puzzle number, a small wordmark) reusing the existing `LevelCompleteEffect`/UI styling will perform better on visual-first platforms than plain text, at a modest one-time art/UI cost.
- **Time the push-notification opt-in ask to the moment right after a player's first Daily Challenge completion** ("Come back tomorrow for Puzzle #X+1") rather than at first app launch — asking right after a completed, positive experience converts opt-in rates far better than a cold permission prompt on install.
- **A future companion web page, held explicitly as a "validate demand first" idea, not a launch commitment:** because `DailySeedGenerator` is a pure, deterministic function of the UTC date, a lightweight static page (e.g., a GitHub Pages site, no server infrastructure needed) could show non-players "today's Time Loop difficulty" or a non-playable preview, giving a shared link somewhere to land other than a cold App Store page. This should not be built or promised before launch — it only becomes worth the (small) engineering cost once organic share volume from the in-app card is actually observed and a real fraction of shares are generating link clicks with nowhere useful to go. Note explicitly in any roadmap conversation that this is a possible v1.x-or-later addition contingent on data, not a committed feature.

## 5. Post-Launch Content Cadence as Marketing Beats

Each phase in `docs/ROADMAP.md`'s post-launch plan is a distinct re-engagement and press moment, not merely a changelog entry:

### v1.1 — Fill the campaign (target ~6-8 weeks post-launch)
Completing the remaining levels toward the full 150+ using the shipped `LevelEditorWindow` (per Roadmap) is the first re-engagement push: send a "23 new levels are live across Worlds 3 and 4" notification specifically to premium owners who finished the shipped slice, refresh App Store/Play screenshots and description copy to reflect the real, final level count, and pitch it to press as a "content completion" story — storefronts' "recently updated" surfaces reward substantive updates, and this is the first one with real news value (going from a proof-of-concept slice to the full advertised campaign).

### v1.2 — Time Variations (Slow Time, Fast Forward, Broken Timeline, Parallel Timeline, Reverse Timeline)
This is the single biggest post-launch marketing beat, because it is new *mechanics*, not just new levels, built on the already-present `LoopRuleModifier` hook on `TimeLoopManager`. Treat it as a second mini-launch: a short trailer reusing the exact "wait" reveal format from §3 but for a new modifier ("wait... it plays backward?" for Reverse Timeline), a second editorial-feature pitch to Apple/Google (substantial updates are re-feature-eligible), and a second press outreach round to the same puzzle-press list from §2 — this is real, comparison-driving news for outlets that already covered launch. It's also the natural moment to publicize that Infinite Mode gets the same modifiers, since the generator architecture applies them uniformly.

### v1.3 — Cosmetic themes (Cyberpunk, Ancient Clockwork, Space-Time, Dream World, Minimal Black & White)
Purely visual, difficulty-neutral content (explicitly non-pay-to-win per GDD §11) is ideal short-form video material: low-spoiler, highly screenshot/GIF-friendly ("5 new looks for your ghost"), and safe to promote broadly without undermining the ethical-monetization positioning that differentiates this game — reiterate explicitly in any coverage that themes are cosmetic-only and never gate content or difficulty. If timing allows, a single theme launch can be nudged toward a loosely relevant seasonal window (e.g., a gothic "Ancient Clockwork" reveal near autumn) for extra short-form pickup, but this should never be forced against the actual content-readiness timeline.

### v1.4 — Community challenges (curated/shareable Infinite Mode seeds + leaderboard)
This is the point where the game gets a genuine user-generated/community loop beyond the Daily Challenge — "Puzzle of the Week" curation, seed-sharing between friend groups, leaderboard bragging rights. Use it to reactivate the Reddit/Discord/social audience built during pre-launch and the v1.2 beat, and frame it publicly as "the game keeps producing new puzzles long after you finish the campaign." Flag internally (and to press, honestly) that this phase depends on a lightweight backend the offline-first v1 does not have (per Roadmap) — set expectations on timing accordingly rather than promising a date before the backend work is scoped.

### Beyond v1.4 — v2.0 player-facing level editor
Worth a brief, low-commitment tease once v1.4 ships (a simplified in-app version of the internal `LevelEditorWindow`), since player-created content is historically one of the strongest long-term goodwill and retention drivers for puzzle games with a small, dedicated design vocabulary (the same reason Baba Is You's and The Witness's communities stayed active years after launch) — treat this as a north-star mention in interviews/AMAs, not a near-term roadmap commitment.

## 6. Pricing Rationale

**Why one-time purchase, $4.99–$7.99, not subscription, not F2P:**

- The target buyer (Monument Valley/The Room/Baba Is You owners) has a proven, well-documented willingness to pay upfront for finite premium puzzle content, and an equally well-documented aversion to subscriptions and energy-timer F2P mechanics in this specific genre — those buyers actively route around games that interrupt the "thinking moment" with monetization, which is precisely the design principle already baked into the product (GDD §11: no ads mid-puzzle, no pay-to-win, no energy systems).
- A subscription model would imply an ongoing content cadence large enough to sustain recurring billing indefinitely; a 150-level campaign plus periodic post-launch packs (§5) is real, valuable content but not subscription-shaped content velocity. Charging a subscription for a product this finite would be a mismatch between business model and actual content shape that this exact, genre-literate audience notices and penalizes in reviews.
- F2P is structurally disqualified by the product's own ethics: with no pay-to-win, no ads during play, and no energy system, the usual F2P monetization levers simply don't exist here — a "free with IAP" wrapper around a product with none of F2P's monetization mechanics reliably underperforms (this is the well-known pattern behind Monument Valley 2's softer numbers relative to the original's straightforward paid model).
- $4.99-$7.99 matches the actual perceived content volume — 150+ campaign levels, a genuinely infinite solver-verified procedural mode, and a forever-free daily puzzle is a multi-week amount of premium content, benchmarked directly against The Room (paid per episode), Monument Valley ($4.99), and Baba Is You ($4.99-$9.99). Pricing below this band (e.g., $0.99) reads as underpricing the content to genre-literate buyers and, in practice, to storefront editorial teams who read very-low pricing as a quality signal in the wrong direction; pricing above it (e.g., $9.99+) shifts buyer expectations toward a fully narrative single-player game and would suppress day-one conversion for a launch with no prior audience or brand recognition to carry a premium anchor.

**What justifies the low end ($4.99) vs. the high end ($6.99-$7.99):**

- **Launch at $4.99.** Day-one purchase velocity is the highest-leverage lever available for algorithmic chart/search visibility on both storefronts, and a studio with zero prior audience has no brand equity to support a premium anchor price on day one. $4.99 also matches the closest direct comp (Monument Valley) almost exactly, minimizing price-anchoring friction for the exact buyer this pitch targets. It should also be framed publicly and honestly as an introductory price tied to the *current* content volume (the launch slice, per Roadmap, is the full engine but a partial campaign) — a "buy now before it's worth more" framing that is legitimate here specifically because it will actually become true.
- **Raise toward $6.99-$7.99 only once the content volume actually changes to justify it** — concretely, after v1.1 ships the full 150+ level campaign (the product now matches what was originally advertised at full scale) and/or after v1.2's Time Variations meaningfully expands total playtime with new mechanics, not new levels alone. A secondary, independent justification to raise price is an accumulated strong review base (a 4.5+ average with a meaningful review count) — social proof that can support a higher anchor without materially hurting conversion, since new buyers are now pricing against visible quality signal rather than blind trust in an unknown studio.
- **Discount tactically, not habitually.** Time-boxed discounts tied to real marketing beats (launch week, a v1.2 major-update relaunch, seasonal storefront sales) are appropriate; frequent or open-ended discounting is not, because this specific audience is not price-sensitive at this band relative to typical mobile spend, and training them to wait for a sale actively damages full-price conversion for a catalog this small.

## 7. Success Metrics

Metrics should be read with the specific shape of this product in mind: it is a *finite* premium campaign wrapped around an *infinite* free daily habit, which means "engagement decline" and "satisfied customer who finished the content" look identical in raw retention curves unless Daily Challenge participation is read alongside them.

**D1 / D7 / D30 retention**
- **D1: 40-55%.** Meaningfully higher than typical F2P casual benchmarks (commonly 25-30%) because every install already passed a purchase-intent filter — nobody accidentally buys a $4.99-$7.99 puzzle game. If launch-week D1 is materially below ~35%, that's a strong first-session-friction signal worth investigating immediately (most likely candidate: the Level 1-01 teaching moment isn't landing without text, which is exactly the risk flagged in GDD §4.1 and the reason beta feedback in §2 specifically probes it).
- **D7: 15-25%.** Read this number together with campaign-completion rate, not alone — a chunk of D7 decline in a finite premium puzzle game is players who finished (or hit) the free-tier wall and are satisfied, not churned. Track "reached free-tier wall and did not purchase" as a separate cohort from "opened once and never returned," since only the latter is an unambiguous problem.
- **D30: 8-15%,** and by this point almost entirely attributable to Daily Challenge habitual opens rather than remaining campaign content for most users (the campaign, even at full 150+ levels, is finishable well inside 30 days for an engaged player). A D30 number in range but with low Daily Challenge participation among those retained users is actually the warning sign to act on — it means people are opening the app for reasons other than the mechanism actually designed to sustain D30, which won't hold.

**Daily Challenge participation rate**
Because Time Loop is a dual-mode game (finite campaign + infinite daily habit), its Daily Challenge participation won't mirror a pure Wordle-style app (which commonly sees 60-80%+ of active users doing the daily puzzle, since it's the *only* thing to do). A realistic target here is **25-40% of daily active users completing that day's challenge**, rising over the game's lifetime as more of the base moves from "still in the campaign" to "campaign finished, daily-challenge-only" mode — track this ratio's trend over months, not just its launch-week absolute value, since it should climb as the early cohort ages into the post-campaign phase. The `Streak` achievement (7 consecutive daily completions, per GDD §9) is a useful proxy metric in its own right: track the percentage of Daily Challenge participants who earn it as a habit-formation signal.

**Free-to-premium conversion**
Blended install-to-purchase conversion for a one-time-unlock mobile puzzle game with this much free content (50 full levels — all of World 1's 20 plus 30 of World 2's — and a forever-free daily puzzle) should realistically land at **2-5% of total installs**, in line with general "generous free sample, hard paywall" premium-unlock benchmarks across mobile. The more actionable number is conversion measured *at the actual decision point* — users who reach the end of World 2's free 30 levels and hit the paywall — where a healthy target is **15-25% of users who reach that specific wall converting**, since that cohort has already self-selected as engaged by clearing 50 levels; a wall-specific conversion rate well below 15% is a pricing-perception or paywall-messaging problem to investigate before touching the base price.

**Pre-registration/pre-order to launch-day conversion**
Treat Apple's Pre-Order and Google Play's pre-registration as separate metrics with different intent signals, not one blended number: Google Play pre-registration campaigns (especially ones tied to a sign-up reward, per §2) typically convert **5-15% of pre-registrants into day-one installs/opens** — reward-driven sign-up can inflate the top of that funnel without proportionally raising true intent. Apple Pre-Order, by contrast, is a single explicit tap by someone who already wants the specific app and auto-downloads with no further action required on release day, so a realistic target is **40-60%+ conversion to day-one install** — track it separately and don't let a strong blended number mask a weak Play funnel or vice versa.

**Instrumentation notes**
None of the above requires new gameplay engineering — the events already exist in the shipped systems described in Technical Architecture §4.8: `SaveSystem`/`SaveData` already tracks per-session opens and dates needed for D1/D7/D30 buckets, `DailyChallengeManager` already gates and records one official completion per day (the exact event needed for participation-rate tracking), and `IAPManager`'s purchase-completion callback is the exact hook needed for conversion timestamps relative to install date and relative to reaching the World-2 paywall. The remaining work is purely an analytics-SDK integration wrapping those existing manager events (`OnLoopEnd`, daily-completion callbacks, purchase callbacks) into whatever privacy-respecting mobile analytics tool is chosen at build-integration time — consistent with the project's offline-first architecture, this should be an additive wrapper, not a redesign of any existing system.
